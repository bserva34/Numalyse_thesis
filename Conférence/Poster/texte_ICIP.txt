Introduction

Hello, my name is Benjamin, and I'll present our work on tag-image extraction for film shots.

In film analysis, the first essential step is shot segmentation. The objective is to identify the editing transitions in order to divide a movie into individual shots. This can be done manually by experts or automatically using shot boundary detection methods. 

The challenge is that a feature film may contain from a few dozen to several hundred shots, making the segmentation difficult to visualize and explore as a whole.

Motivation

A natural solution is to associate one representative image with each shot.

However, this image must summarize the semantic and narrative content of the shot despite the large visual diversity found in cinema. We introduced the term tag-image to distinguish it from keyframes, which generally refer to a set of images representing the key stages of an action or movement. 

Method

Our method represents each frame of a shot with a semantic histogram built from the objects detected by an object detector. Each histogram contains one bin per object category. 


We then compute the average histogram of the shot and select the frame whose histogram is the closest to this average. The intuition is that this frame is the most representative of the overall shot content. 


To improve the selection, we introduce three weighting strategies inspired by cinematic composition:

Sharpness weighting favors visually sharp frames.

Centered spatial weighting gives more importance to objects located near the center of the frame.

Temporal weighting favors frames occurring around the middle or the end of the shot, where the narrative climax is more likely to appear. 


Experimental evaluation

We compare our approach with four baseline methods: the naïve Middle Frame approach and three methods from the literature. All competing methods were adapted to produce a single representative frame for a fair comparison.

Expert evaluation

To validate these observations, we conducted a study with 16 film-analysis experts on 10 movie shots.

The experts selected every image they considered representative. Our method achieved the highest selection rate, 51.3%, and was ranked Top-1 in 50% of the evaluated shots, clearly outperforming the other approaches. 


Large-scale evaluation

We also performed a quantitative evaluation on 105 annotated shots.

Using the Bhattacharyya distance between the selected image and the expert reference, our method obtained the lowest mean distance and the lowest standard deviation, showing both higher accuracy and better robustness. 


Conclusion

To conclude, we proposed a semantic histogram-based method for extracting one representative tag-image per film shot. By integrating spatial, temporal, and sharpness weightings inspired by cinematic rules, our approach is consistently preferred by film-analysis experts.

As future work, we plan to improve the semantic representation of the histograms using more accurate object detection models.
