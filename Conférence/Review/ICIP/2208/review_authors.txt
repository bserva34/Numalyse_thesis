1) Strengths

This paper proposes a spatio-frequency cooperative fusion network (SFCFNet) for multimodal medical image fusion, combining processing in both the frequency and spatial domains. This approach is well-founded and addresses a significant shortcoming of existing methods, which operate primarily in the spatial domain.

The manuscript has several notable strengths:

-Reproducibility and clarity:
The implementation details, training procedure, and experimental setup are well described. This significantly improves the reproducibility of the proposed method.

-Comprehensive experimental evaluation:
The method is evaluated on several datasets (MRI-CT, MRI-PET, MRI-SPECT), with comparisons against a wide range of state-of-the-art methods. This strengthens the empirical validation.

-Use of multiple evaluation metrics:
The use of four metrics (AG, SF, VIF, QAB/F) provides a reasonably comprehensive quantitative evaluation.

-Well-designed ablation study:
The ablation experiments clearly demonstrate the contribution of the frequency interaction (FI) and spatial compensation (SC) modules in the three medical fusion tasks. This justifies the architectural choices.


2)Weaknesses and Suggestions

Although the article is generally sound, several points warrant attention to improve its quality and scientific rigor:

1 - Lack of references for certain evaluation metrics

The manuscript uses standard evaluation metrics (AG, SF, VIF, QAB/F).
The AG and SF metrics can be considered to have been introduced via the review [1]. However, no references are provided for the definition of the VIF and QAB/F metrics.

For VIF, this is a well-established perceptual indicator whose original publication is clearly identified:
Yu Han et al., “A new image fusion performance metric based on visual information fidelity”.

Similarly, the QAB/F metric has a well-defined origin in the literature on image fusion and could also be properly referenced.

2. Error in Table 1 (VIF metric)

There appears to be an inconsistency in Table 1 regarding the VIF metric.
The identification of the best and second-best methods seems incorrect, as the values appear to be reversed.
